About — who is behind SEKTOR_X

Updated: 27.09.2026

Online security in plain language, for regular people and for small businesses — written by an ordinary sysadmin who does it every day, plus my journal from the road through IT. No editorial team, no sponsors, no fluff.

What SEKTOR_X is

SEKTOR_X is two things at once. On one side, a guide for regular people and small businesses: concrete, verifiable instructions — from passwords, phishing and your home router to firewalls, Active Directory, backup, email and NIS2 — written Polish-first and without corporate jargon. On the other, my journal — the Operator Base: notes on what I'm building, what I'm learning and where I got it wrong.

The two are connected. Most guides start as a journal note (“set up Samba as a domain controller today and DNS refused to cooperate”) and only after a few attempts turn into a step-by-step walkthrough.

Who writes it

One person, no editorial team, no sponsor. I am an ordinary sysadmin at one company — from FortiGate and Active Directory to the NIS2 rollout I am running right now. I know the absurdities of small firms getting corporate-grade requirements with a “find your own budget” attitude. I write about what I configure, break and fix myself. I document the journey so that a year from now I can see where I started — and so that someone at the start of the same road knows they're not alone.

The site is written in Polish first. Some articles are translated into English; the interactive admin guides are Polish-only for now.

Principles

  • Verifiable. Facts come with sources. If I haven't tested something myself, I say so.
  • Up to date. Every article shows its publication date and last update. Security advice ages fast.
  • Independent. Nobody pays for a spot in a ranking. If affiliate links ever appear, they will be clearly marked and won't change the recommendations.
  • Private. No ads, no trackers, no tracking cookies. Details in the privacy policy.
  • Corrections. Found a mistake? Tell me. I fix it and update the date.

Contact

Easiest way: subscribe to the newsletter and reply to any email — it comes straight to me. You can also report a mistake or suggest a topic on GitHub.
