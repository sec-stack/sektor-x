10 Things You Must Do Today To Avoid Getting Hacked in 2026

09.05.202510 min read

Password manager, 2FA, VPN, updates — sounds boring, but each step genuinely reduces your risk by 80%. A concrete checklist with no filler.

Most hacks don't happen through advanced movie-style exploits. They happen through your 2018 password that leaked from some site and still works on your bank account. Here are 10 steps you can take today — no technical knowledge required.

1. Install a Password Manager

This is the absolute foundation. A password manager generates and stores unique, random passwords for every service. You only remember one — the master password.

  • Bitwarden — free, open-source, works on all devices — audited annually by firms including Cure53 and Mandiant
  • 1Password — paid (~$3.99/month — price increased 33% in March 2026), great UX, recommended for teams and families
  • KeePass — free, local (no cloud), for advanced users
💡 How to start: Download Bitwarden (bitwarden.com), create an account, install the browser extension. Next time you log in anywhere — save the password and replace it with a random one.

2. Enable Two-Factor Authentication (2FA)

Even if someone gets your password, without the second factor they can't get in. 2FA is an extra code generated on your phone.

  • Install Aegis Authenticator (Android) or 2FAS (iOS/Android) — Raivo OTP changed ownership and has trust issues
  • Enable 2FA on: Gmail, Facebook, Instagram, your bank, Amazon
  • Avoid SMS-based 2FA — it's weaker than an app

3. Check If Your Data Has Leaked

Go to haveibeenpwned.com and enter your email. The site checks if your data appeared in known breaches. If it did — change passwords for those services immediately.

⚠️ Warning: If you reuse the same password across multiple services and it leaked — change it everywhere, not just in one place.

4. Update Your System and Apps

Most ransomware and exploits use known vulnerabilities in unpatched software. Security patches are released precisely to be installed.

  • Windows: Settings → Windows Update → Check for updates
  • Mac: System Settings → General → Software Update
  • Phone: Settings → About → System updates
  • Enable automatic updates — don't postpone them

5. Secure Your Home Router

Your router is the gateway to your entire network. Default credentials are publicly listed in databases hackers actively use.

  • Change the admin password (not the WiFi password — the router admin panel password)
  • Update router firmware
  • Disable WPS — it's a known vulnerability
  • Set WPA3 if your router supports it — or WPA3/WPA2 Mixed mode if you have older devices (smart TV, printers). Never WPA or WEP

6. Use a VPN on Public WiFi

Coffee shops, airports, hotels — public WiFi is an open door for attackers. A VPN encrypts your traffic so nobody on the same network can intercept it.

💡 Recommended: Mullvad (best for privacy — police came for data, left empty-handed), ProtonVPN (free tier, Swiss jurisdiction), NordVPN (popular, easy to use, 6 no-logs audits — note ownership ties to a data-scraping company). Avoid free VPNs — they often sell your data.

7. Encrypt Your Hard Drive

If your laptop is stolen, encryption means the thief can't read your files without your password.

  • Windows: Search "BitLocker" — enable it
  • Mac: System Settings → Privacy & Security → FileVault → Turn On (Macs with Apple Silicon encrypt automatically — FileVault adds password protection at startup)
  • This is a one-time setup that runs silently in the background

8. Review App Permissions

That flashlight app doesn't need access to your contacts and location. Audit which apps have access to what.

  • Phone: Settings → Apps → Permissions
  • Revoke access to camera, microphone, location for apps that don't need it

9. Use a Separate Email for Signups

Create a secondary email address for newsletters, account registrations, and anything non-critical. Your primary email stays clean and harder to target.

10. Back Up Your Data

Ransomware encrypts your files and demands payment. The only real defense is backups.

  • Follow the 3-2-1 rule: 3 copies, 2 different media, 1 offsite
  • Cloud backup: Backblaze (~$9/month for unlimited data — Windows and Mac only), Proton Drive, or iDrive + external drive
  • Test your backups — a backup you've never restored is just hope
