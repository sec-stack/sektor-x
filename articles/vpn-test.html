Which VPN Actually Doesn't Log? We Tested 12 Services

06.05.20268 min read

Every VPN claims 'no logs'. But when court orders arrived — some proved otherwise. We check which services actually confirmed it under real-world pressure.

The VPN market is full of marketing promises. "Military-grade encryption." "Absolute no-logs." "Your data is safe with us." Then a court order arrives and some providers hand over IP logs they claimed didn't exist.

Our Evaluation Criteria

  • Audited no-log policy — independent third-party audit published
  • Legal track record — responded to law enforcement requests?
  • Jurisdiction — country of incorporation and applicable laws
  • Open source client — can the code be verified?
  • Payment anonymity — cash / Monero accepted?

Results — Comparison Table

After reviewing privacy policies, audit reports, and documented legal cases:

  • Mullvad — Sweden, audited, accepts cash/crypto, no account email required. When raided by Swedish police in 2023, had zero user data to hand over. August 2025 audit (Assured Security Consultants) — no critical vulnerabilities found. ✅ Top pick
  • ProtonVPN — Switzerland, open source, audited, free tier. Handed over IP in a 2019 case per Swiss law (email service, not VPN) — important nuance. ✅ Recommended
  • IVPN — Gibraltar, audited, accepts Monero, minimal account data. Smaller service, excellent track record. ✅ Solid choice
  • ExpressVPN — BVI (owned by Kape Technologies, formerly Crossrider — an adware company). In 2017 their Turkey server aided a murder investigation. Kape also owns CyberGhost and PIA — the same company behind many "independent" VPN review sites. 🚨 Avoid
  • NordVPN — Panama*, popular, 6 no-logs audits including Deloitte (December 2025). Note: ownership ties to Tesonet, a Lithuanian firm that also owns Oxylabs (data scraping). No evidence of data sharing, but worth knowing. Ongoing class-action lawsuits over auto-renewal practices (filed 2024 and 2025). ⚠️ Good but check ownership
🚨 Avoid: Any VPN offering "lifetime subscriptions" for $20, free VPNs with no clear business model, and VPNs based in the US, UK, Australia, Canada, or New Zealand (Five Eyes countries — intelligence sharing agreement).

Top 3 Recommendation

  • Mullvad — best for maximum anonymity. No email, pay with cash or Monero. Police raid proved no-logs (2023). €5/month (~$5.50), one plan, unlimited devices.
  • ProtonVPN — best balance of privacy and usability. Swiss jurisdiction, free tier with no data cap, open-source. Note: in 2021 Proton Mail handed over an activist's IP to Swiss authorities — VPN operates under different rules, but worth knowing.
  • IVPN — best for advanced users. Gibraltar jurisdiction (outside 14 Eyes), accepts Monero, audited, no email required.
⚠️ NordVPN caveat: 6 independent no-logs audits (latest: Deloitte, December 2025) confirm no logging. However, NordVPN has ownership ties to Tesonet — a Lithuanian firm that also owns Oxylabs (a data scraping company). No evidence of data sharing, but privacy-focused users should know. Also: ongoing class-action lawsuits over deceptive auto-renewal practices (filed 2024 and 2025). *Panama is the legal jurisdiction; operations run from the Netherlands and Lithuania.

Surfshark note: Merged with Nord Security (NordVPN's parent) in 2022 — despite separate brands, it's now one company.

What to Avoid

🚨 Red flags: ExpressVPN was acquired by Kape Technologies (formerly Crossrider — an adware firm). In 2017 their Turkey server aided a murder investigation. Kape also owns CyberGhost and Private Internet Access — the same company behind many supposedly "independent" VPN review sites. Avoid.

Free VPNs without exception log and sell your data. That's their business model. If you're not paying, you are the product.

What VPNs Won't Protect You From

A VPN is not a silver bullet. It does not:

  • Protect against malware on your device
  • Hide your identity if you're logged into Google while using it
  • Protect against browser fingerprinting
  • Anonymize you on social media
💡 VPN is one layer: Use it alongside a password manager, 2FA, and updated software. Defense in depth.
