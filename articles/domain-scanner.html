// What we check and why it matters

Email was designed at a time when nobody thought about fraudsters — by default anyone can send an email "from" your company. That's how most CEO fraud and fake invoices with a swapped bank account number work. Three DNS records fix this:

SPF
A list of servers allowed to send email on behalf of the domain.
DKIM
A digital signature on every email — the recipient checks that the content hasn't been altered and that it really comes from you.
DMARC
Instructions for receiving servers: what to do with an email that fails SPF/DKIM — let it through, send it to spam or reject it. Without it, SPF and DKIM are just suggestions.
HTTPS
An encrypted connection to your website. Without it, browsers show a "Not secure" warning.
DNSSEC
Signs your DNS records, so a resolver can detect a tampered answer and won't send you to a fake server.
CAA
Specifies which certificate authorities may issue an SSL certificate for your domain.

Since 2024 Gmail and Yahoo, and since May 2025 also Outlook.com, require SPF, DKIM and DMARC from bulk senders, and at least SPF or DKIM from everyone else. Without them, your invoices and offers increasingly end up in spam. It's not just security, it's deliverability too.
